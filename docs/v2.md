# Agent Theatre v2 — what would make it *fun*

v1 proves the pipeline: events → beats → a lit stage with a character who goes where the
work is. The honest critique of v1: it is legible but *polite*. The character walks, taps,
and tests pass. Nothing surprises you. v2 is about surprise, stakes and keepsakes.

## 1. Export the performance (the growth loop)

- **`agent-theatre export <session>`** → an `.mp4`/`.gif`/`.webm` of the replay, rendered
  headlessly at fixed step (we already capture deterministically with `?step=`). Options:
  `--set`, `--cast`, `--from/--to`, `--size 1080p|square|vertical`, `--captions`.
- **Share card**: a 1200×630 poster of the finale (stage, lead, evidence chip, title) for a
  PR description or a tweet. `--poster`.
- **Session trailer**: a 15-second auto-cut (arrive → trouble → fix → finale) for long
  sessions, chosen by beat salience (first fail, last pass, longest build).
- Link from the player: **Export** button next to Set.

## 2. Stakes: things can actually go wrong on stage

- **Gremlins have a life.** They multiply on repeated failures, build little nests on the
  file they broke, steal the beacon's lamp (it goes dark until the test passes), and
  *flee to the dock* when banished — one wave goodbye from the boat.
- **Weather follows mood.** `lost` brings fog that hides the far districts; a long retry
  loop brings rain; `happy` after `lost` brings the sun out with a rainbow over the
  beacons. Weather is a renderer-only layer; the Director already emits mood.
- **The forge can misfire.** A failed command throws sparks and a puff of black smoke; a
  retry rekindles it. Three failures in a row and the character puts on a helmet.
- **The claim check as a scene.** When the agent claims success with no evidence, instead
  of the gremlin merely hiding, the beacon keeper walks out, holds up a lantern to the
  character, and the gremlin is revealed in the light — then the evidence chip reads
  "unverified". It is the product's thesis as a 4-second gag.

## 3. Character: a cast that acts, not just walks

- **Idle business.** Idle time is where personality lives: Pip juggles pebbles, Mo reads a
  newspaper, Sol does a little dance, Bolt paces, Nimbus floats and drifts, Ember kicks the
  forge. One procedural gag per cast member, triggered after 3 s of idle.
- **Reactions.** On test fail: Bolt facepalms, Rook adjusts its visor, Juno says "!" and
  runs. On pass: Sol cartwheels, Ivy waters the beacon, Tove salutes.
- **Walk cycles with intent.** Running when late (gap compression > 2×), trudging when
  `lost`, strutting after a pass. Cheap: speed + bob amplitude + lean per mood.
- **Helpers have a job.** Subagents row the boat in, carry a crate to the district they
  were sent to, and bring back a scroll to the lead when they finish.

## 4. The stage itself is alive

- **Time of day** advances with the session: dawn → noon → golden hour → night, with the
  windows lighting up and beacons getting brighter as night falls. A 2-hour session feels
  like a day.
- **Traffic.** Birds, boats, a tram in Metropolis, a drone in Orbit, lanterns rising in
  Lanterns — one per stage, on a slow loop, never where the action is.
- **Growth.** A file edited many times in one session gets a small flag; a new file keeps
  scaffolding for a while; a deleted file leaves a plot of grass.
- **Camera as a director.** A slow push-in on the beacon during a test run; a whip-pan to
  a gremlin spawn; a crane-up for the finale. Shot grammar per beat, not one follow cam.

## 5. Play

- **Click a building** to see its event history for this session; **click the character**
  to see its mood over time; **hover a beacon** for the last test summary.
- **Director's commentary** toggle: one-line captions that explain *why* the character is
  doing something ("Two failures in 90 s — Pip is lost").
- **Episode titles** generated from the session: "The Flaky Test", "Forty Reads and an
  Edit", "The Helper Who Found It".
- **Achievements** (local, never sent anywhere): "Green first time", "Banished 5 gremlins",
  "Night shift" (session past midnight). Shown on the end card.

## 6. Sound as score

- The jingle becomes a **theme**: a 4-bar motif per stage that the finale resolves.
- **Leitmotifs**: a two-note sting per cast member, played when they arrive.
- **Dynamic score**: ambience thins when `lost`, swells on `celebrate`, a low drone under a
  long test run. All still synthesized.

## Order

Export (1) first — it is the growth loop and reuses the fixed-step capture we already
have. Then stakes (2) and idle business (3): those change how it *feels* most per hour of
work. Time of day (4) and play (5) after. Score (6) alongside, since it is cheap.
